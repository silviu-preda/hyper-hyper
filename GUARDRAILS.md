# hyper-hyper — Project Goal

## Purpose

To reconstruct and explore the European rave ecosystem of roughly 1986–1998 through the artists named in Scooter's *Hyper Hyper* roll-call.

The central idea is **not primarily to study Scooter, nor to make a collection of the best rave music**. It's to understand the **world they were saluting**.

## What We're Trying to Capture

- **The 30 artists named in *Hyper Hyper* are our initial cast.**
- We trace **each artist's musical evolution**, rather than assigning them a single genre or picking one famous track.
- The period is roughly **1986–1998**, allowing us to see where these artists came from and what they became.
- We deliberately cross **genre boundaries**: techno, house, hardcore, trance, breakbeat, jungle, D&B, gabber, acid, etc.
- We pay particular attention to **cross-pollination between scenes and countries**.
- We look at the **people and infrastructure behind the music**: DJs, clubs, raves, labels, promoters, radio, touring networks and scenes.
- We are interested in **the culture of rave as much as the recordings** — particularly the extraordinary sense of collective identity and allegiance that appears repeatedly in the period.

## The Central Historical Question

Something like:

> **What was the European rave ecosystem that these artists inhabited, and how did it evolve from the mid-1980s to the end of the 1990s?**

## Distinction from *Jungle Across the World*

*Jungle Across the World* asks:

> *Where did jungle exist, and what did its ecosystem look like?*

*Hyper Hyper* asks:

> *What was the larger European rave ecosystem from which these different musical worlds emerged, collided and evolved?*

## The Playlist Is a Research Instrument

We're not trying to produce a conventional “best of the 30 artists.”

We want to construct a **musical historical journey**, where tracks are selected because they tell us something about:

- an artist's evolution;
- a scene;
- a stylistic mutation;
- a crossover;
- a technological development;
- a cultural connection;
- or simply because **it's fucking essential**.

## A Core Cultural Lens

We are particularly interested in:

> **DJ ↔ crowd rather than artist → audience.**

The *Love Parade*, marathon DJ sets, Mayday, free parties, European touring networks, Scooter's repeated invocation of the **European posse**, and jungle's own rejection of social and genre boundaries are all potentially pieces of the same larger story.

## The Real Subject

The repo isn't ultimately about **30 DJs**.

It's about **the culture that made “30 DJs” a meaningful roll-call in the first place.**
